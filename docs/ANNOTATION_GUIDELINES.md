ANNOTATION GUIDELINES: SARCASM IN MARATHI-ENGLISH CODE-MIXED TEXT (version 1.0, pilot draft)

WHAT YOU ARE DOING
You will read short sentences written by social-media users, in Marathi and English mixed together, in Roman or Devanagari script. For each sentence choose one label in the drop-down: sarcastic, not sarcastic, or cannot tell.
Work alone. Do not discuss any label with teammates until everyone has finished a batch. Independent judgements are the whole point.

WHEN TO CHOOSE "sarcastic"
Choose it when the writer most plausibly means the opposite of what the words literally say, or mocks someone or something through apparent praise or agreement.
Typical patterns: praise for something bad ("great, another power cut"), fake gratitude, exaggerated enthusiasm about something annoying, a compliment used as an insult.

WHEN TO CHOOSE "not sarcastic"
The sentence means what it says. This includes sincere praise, sincere complaints, plain facts, questions, greetings, and jokes that do not rely on saying the opposite.

WHEN TO CHOOSE "cannot tell"
You would need information that is not in the sentence (a news event, a person, a picture), or two sensible readers could read it opposite ways. Use this honestly and do not guess. We count how often it is used.

EDGE CASES
- Negative sentiment alone is not sarcasm. "This bus service is terrible" is a sincere complaint.
- Laughing emoji or "lol" alone do not make a sentence sarcastic.
- Irony of a situation is not sarcasm unless the writer speaks ironically about it.
- Rhetorical questions are sarcastic only when the intended answer is the opposite of the literal one.
- Quoted sarcasm, reported speech and song lines: judge the writer's own meaning.
- Hashtags are part of the text. Do not search for the source tweet.
- If a sentence is offensive or upsetting, you may skip it: leave it blank and write "skip" in the note column.

EXAMPLES (written for these guidelines; native readers should replace or confirm them with real examples from the pilot)
- sarcastic: "Wow, great job, the bus left on time for once, ha." (English, said when the bus was late)
- sarcastic: "व्वा, पुन्हा लाईट गेली, किती छान!" (Wow, the power is gone again, how nice!)
- sarcastic: "wah, phir se traffic mein fas gaye, mazaa aa gaya" (Hinglish: stuck in traffic again, what fun)
- not sarcastic: "आज हवामान छान आहे." (The weather is nice today.)
- not sarcastic: "Traffic was terrible today, reached office very late." (a sincere complaint)
- cannot tell: "Nice move by the minister." (depends on who and what)

PROCESS
1. Pilot: label the 100 pilot sentences. Then the team meets, compares the pilot labels, rewrites unclear rules, and the pilot items are discarded from the final set.
2. Final: label the final workbook in sessions of at most 45 minutes, with breaks. Note your start and end times.
3. Save your workbook after every session. Return your own workbook only. Never edit other people's.
4. Do not look anything up and do not use translation or AI tools to decide a label. We want your own judgement.
